What gets witnessed, what gets remembered, what stays withheld.

I build mechanisms around feelings. Each one holds a specific emotion the way a house holds a person.

I.

I. Witnessed

A life can be completely observable and completely unwitnessed at the same time. Most are, most of the time. So the works often meter their generosity to presence: stay with them and they give more; leave and they carry on without you. A figure who lifts his head only when someone has stopped to look. An image that surfaces only for a viewer willing to be silent. Anyone can watch. What turns watching into witness?

II.

II. Remembered

The works keep dates. A candle appears one evening a year. An upside-down image flips for a single day, and the world rights itself. A color completes its circuit in nineteen hours and twenty-nine minutes, in honor of a birth year. These are vigils, and machines turn out to be good at them. They don’t skip a year, don’t get tired, don’t decide a grief has gone on long enough. One work records nothing at all, by promise. Another remembers only whoever holds it. If a machine keeps the vigil, does the vigil still count?

III.

III. Withheld

The works keep things. Details almost no one will ever find. Dates I don’t announce. Questions I answer nowhere. None of it is a puzzle with a prize. What the works withhold was never owed to anyone. The other half of that bargain is legibility: I will always name the medium, and I have credited AI in the work since 2023. When a work keeps a secret, is it keeping it from you, or for someone?
